Expressions, equations and formulas

Sessions 6–7  |  21 – 25 September  |  Worksheets 6 and 7

A letter in algebra is a short name for a number you do not know yet, or one that changes. A formula is a rule you already follow, written down. Solving an equation is working backwards to find the number — keeping both sides balanced as you go.

3.1 Letters for numbers

Seed costs $4 a kilogram. How much do you pay?

It depends how much you buy. Call the number of kilograms n, and the cost is 4n dollars. That is an expression: a rule that gives the answer once you know n.

Naming the parts of 4x + 7y − 9

Terms: 4x, 7y and −9 — the pieces separated by + or −. There are three.

Coefficients: 4 and 7 — the numbers in front of the letters.

Variables: x and y.

Constant: −9 — the number on its own. The sign in front belongs to the term.

3 × y is written 3y. And x on its own means 1x. If x = 5, then 2x means 2 × 5 = 10, not 25.

3.2 Collecting like terms

3 bags of maize and 5 bags of maize make 8 bags of maize.

But 3 bags of maize and 2 bags of soya do not make 5 of anything. Like terms are the same letter to the same power; only those can be added.

Collect each kind separately

4a + 3b + 2a − b

= (4a + 2a) + (3b − b) Keep the sign that sits in front of each term.

= 6a + 2b

Powers are different kinds

2x2 + 3x − x2 + x = x2 + 4x x2 and x are as different as maize and soya.

3.3 Substitution

A formula is only useful once you put numbers into it.

Substitution means replacing each letter by its value. Three lines every time: the formula, the formula with numbers in, the answer.

Three lines

If x = 3, find 4x + 5.

Line 1: 4x + 5

Line 2: 4 × 3 + 5

Line 3: 17

Negative values go in brackets

If a = −3, find a2.

(−3)2 = (−3) × (−3) = 9 Without the bracket a calculator gives −9, which is wrong.

3.4 Expanding brackets

Three fields, each needing $40 of fertiliser and $25 of seed.

In Unit 1 you saw that 3($40 + $25) = 3 × $40 + 3 × $25. With letters it is the same move: the number outside multiplies everything inside.

Multiply every term inside

3(x + 4) = 3 × x + 3 × 4 = 3x + 12

−2(x + 5) = −2 × x + (−2) × 5 = −2x − 10 The minus goes onto both terms.

4(y − 2) + 3y = 4y − 8 + 3y = 7y − 8 Expand first, then collect.

3.5 Solving equations by balance

3 bags plus a 4 kg scoop weigh 19 kg. How heavy is one bag?

Take the scoop off: 3 bags weigh 15 kg. Share between 3: one bag is 5 kg. You have just solved 3x + 4 = 19.

Undo, keeping both sides level

3x + 4 = 19

Subtract 4 from both sides: 3x = 15 Whatever you do to one side, do to the other.

Divide both sides by 3: x = 5

Check: 3(5) + 4 = 19 ✓ Always put your answer back in.

3.6 Brackets, and the unknown on both sides

When x appears on both sides, gather it on one side.

Move the smaller amount of x across by subtracting it from both sides. Then it is an ordinary equation.

Worked example

7x − 5 = 4x + 10

Subtract 4x from both sides: 3x − 5 = 10

Add 5 to both sides: 3x = 15

Divide by 3: x = 5

Check: 7(5) − 5 = 30 and 4(5) + 10 = 30 ✓

With brackets: expand first

2(x + 4) = 3x

2x + 8 = 3x

Subtract 2x: 8 = x

3.7 Equations with fractions

A fraction in an equation is a division waiting to be undone.

Get the fraction on its own, then multiply both sides by the bottom number.

Worked example

x ÷ 5 + 2 = 6

Subtract 2 from both sides: x ÷ 5 = 4

Multiply both sides by 5: x = 20

Check: 20 ÷ 5 + 2 = 6 ✓

Two fractions

x ÷ 2 + x ÷ 3 = 10 Multiply every term by 6, which both 2 and 3 go into.

3x + 2x = 60

5x = 60, so x = 12

3.8 Rearranging formulas

You know the area of a plot and its length. How wide is it?

A = lw gives the area. To get the width instead, rearrange the formula with the same balance moves as an equation — you just keep letters instead of numbers.

Make w the subject of A = lw

A = lw

Divide both sides by l: w = A ÷ l Exactly what you would do if l were a number.

Make w the subject of P = 2(l + w)

Divide both sides by 2: P ÷ 2 = l + w

Subtract l: w = P ÷ 2 − l

3.9 Turning a farm problem into an equation

A farmer buys bags of feed at $38 each and the bill is $494. How many bags?

Four steps every time: say what the letter stands for, write the equation, solve it, answer in a sentence.

The four steps

Let b be the number of bags.

38b = 494

b = 494 ÷ 38 = 13

Thirteen bags were bought.

Worksheet 7 · Section E — Word problems on the farm

For each one: say what your letter stands for and give its units, write the equation, solve it, then answer in a full sentence.

  1. A farmer buys bags of feed at $38 each and the total bill is $494. How many bags were bought?

    Show a hint

    Let b be the number of bags. Each costs $38 and the total is $494.

  2. A rectangular plot is 12 metres longer than it is wide. The perimeter is 96 metres. Find the length and the width.

    Show a hint

    Let w be the width, so the length is w + 12. Perimeter = 2(length + width).

  3. A farmer keeps three times as many chickens as goats, and has 96 animals altogether. How many of each does he keep?

    Show a hint

    Let g be the number of goats, so there are 3g chickens. Together they make 96.

  4. Fencing costs a fixed charge of $85 plus $12 for every metre. The total bill is $685. How many metres of fencing were bought?

    Show a hint

    The fixed charge plus $12 for each metre makes $685.

  5. A farmer sells produce at $5 per kilogram. After paying costs of $340 he is left with $1,160. How many kilograms did he sell?

    Show a hint

    Money from sales minus $340 leaves $1,160.

  6. Three fields cover 45 hectares in total. The second field is twice the size of the first, and the third is 5 hectares larger than the first. Find the size of each field.

    Show a hint

    Let f be the first field. The second is 2f and the third is f + 5. Together they make 45.

Watch out for these

Collecting unlike terms
3x + 2y is not 5xy, and x2 + x is not x3. Only the same letter to the same power can be added.
Substituting a negative without brackets
If a = −3, then a2 = (−3)2 = 9, not −9.
Multiplying only the first term in a bracket
3(x + 4) = 3x + 12, not 3x + 4.
Losing the sign in front of a bracket
−2(x + 5) = −2x − 10. The minus goes onto both terms.
Doing something to one side only
An equation is a balance. Subtract, add, multiply or divide on both sides, every time.
Not checking the answer
Put your value back into the original equation. If the two sides are not equal, find the line where it went wrong.

Before you move on

You should be able to collect like terms, substitute (with brackets for negatives), expand a bracket, solve a linear equation and check it, rearrange a formula, and turn a word problem into an equation.
