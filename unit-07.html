7.1 What a matrix is

A feed table: two rations down the side, three ingredients across the top.

Put the numbers in a bracket without the labels and you have a matrix. Its order is rows × columns.

Order and elements

C has 2 rows and 3 columns: order 2 × 3. Rows first, always — like coordinates, the order is fixed.

The number in row 2, column 1 of C is 4.

I, with 1s down the diagonal and 0s elsewhere, is the identity. It behaves like the number 1.

7.2 Adding, subtracting and scaling

Two months of feed orders, in two tables of the same shape.

To get the total, add the numbers in matching positions. That only works if the tables are the same shape — the same order.

Adding: matching positions

A + B:  2 + 5 = 7,  1 + 0 = 1,  3 + (−1) = 2,  4 + 2 = 6

A + B = 7126 A + C cannot be done: 2 × 2 and 2 × 3 do not match.

7.4 Polynomials: the names

A field is (x + 5) metres long and (x + 2) metres wide.

Its area works out to x2 + 7x + 10. An expression made of powers of x like that is a polynomial.

Naming 4x3 − 2x + 7

Three terms: a trinomial. One term: monomial. Two: binomial.

Degree 3 — the highest power.

Leading coefficient 4 — the number in front of the highest power.

Standard form: highest power first. 3 − 2x + 6x2 is written 6x2 − 2x + 3.

7.5 Adding and subtracting polynomials

Collect like terms — exactly as in Unit 3.

The only new danger is subtracting a whole bracket: the minus sign changes the sign of every term inside.

Subtracting a bracket

(3x2 + 2x − 5) − (x2 − 4x + 1)

= 3x2 + 2x − 5 − x2 + 4x − 1 All three signs in the second bracket change.

= 2x2 + 6x − 6

7.6 Multiplying polynomials

Every term in the first bracket multiplies every term in the second.

That one sentence covers every case — a number times a bracket, two brackets, or a bracket of three terms.

(x + 5)(x + 2)

= x(x + 2) + 5(x + 2) Split the first bracket and multiply each part.

= x2 + 2x + 5x + 10

= x2 + 7x + 10

A square bracket

(x + 4)2 = (x + 4)(x + 4)

= x2 + 4x + 4x + 16 = x2 + 8x + 16 Not x2 + 16. The middle term is where the marks are.

7.7 Factorising: taking out a common factor

Factorising is multiplying out in reverse.

You are given 6x2 + 9x and asked: what was multiplied to make this? Look for what every term shares.

6x2 + 9x

Both terms contain 3 and x. Take out 3x. Take the biggest factor every term has.

6x2 + 9x = 3x(2x + 3)

Check: 3x × 2x + 3x × 3 = 6x2 + 9x ✓ Always multiply back out.

7.8 Factorising trinomials

x2 + 7x + 10 came from (x + 5)(x + 2).

Notice: 5 × 2 = 10 and 5 + 2 = 7. To factorise, find two numbers that multiply to the last number and add to the middle one.

x2 + 2x − 15

Multiply to −15, add to 2: 5 and −3. A negative product means one number is negative.

= (x + 5)(x − 3)

Check: x2 − 3x + 5x − 15 ✓

When x2 has a number in front: 2x2 + 7x + 3

a × c = 2 × 3 = 6. Multiply to 6, add to 7: 6 and 1. Use the two numbers to split the middle term.

2x2 + 6x + x + 3 = 2x(x + 3) + 1(x + 3)

= (2x + 1)(x + 3)

7.9 Difference of two squares

A square field of side x with a 5 m by 5 m corner fenced off.

The area left is x2 − 25. Two squares with a minus between them always factorise the same way.

x2 − 25

= (x + 5)(x − 5)

Check: x2 − 5x + 5x − 25 = x2 − 25 ✓ The middle terms cancel.

x2 + 25 does not factorise this way. It must be a difference — a minus.

2x2 − 50 = 2(x2 − 25) = 2(x + 5)(x − 5) Common factor first, then keep going.

Watch out for these

Adding matrices of different orders
Only matrices of exactly the same shape can be added or subtracted.
Multiplying matrices position by position
Matrix multiplication is row times column, multiply and add. Matching positions is only for adding.
Assuming AB = BA
Order matters for matrices. AB and BA are usually different, and one may not even exist.
Forgetting to change every sign when subtracting a bracket
−(x2 − 4x + 1) is −x2 + 4x − 1. All three signs change.
Writing (x + 3)2 = x2 + 9
Test with x = 1: 16 against 10. The correct answer is x2 + 6x + 9.
Cancelling across a plus sign
(x2 + 2x) ÷ x is x + 2, not x2 + 2. Factorise first, then cancel.
Stopping too early
2x3 + 10x2 + 12x = 2x(x + 2)(x + 3). If a bracket still factorises, keep going.
