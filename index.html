MAT1001

College Algebra

School of Agriculture  |  Associate Degree in Agriculture, Year 1  |  Semester 1, 2026–2027

Course outline — what is covered, how you are assessed, and the calendar

Three calculations you will meet on a farm. None of them needs anything you have not already done — only a way of writing it down.

  • A field of 2.5 hectares takes fertiliser at 180 kg per hectare. How many 50 kg bags do you order?

    Rates, multiplication, division

  • Twenty-four animals eat 3.2 kg each a day. Feed costs $1.85 a kilogram. What is the weekly bill?

    Decimals, repeated multiplication, units

  • You sell 900 kg at $4.20 a kilogram and your costs were $2,600. What is the profit, and what is it as a percentage of what you spent?

    Subtraction, percentages, comparison

By December all three are routine, and so is a good deal more. Work through the units below at your own pace — each one has worked examples, practice, and the answers so you can check yourself.

The units

Unit 1

Number systems and operations

What the different kinds of number are, how the four operations behave, and working confidently with signed numbers, fractions, decimals and percentages.

Ready  ·  Sessions 1–3  ·  Worksheet 3

Unit 2

Exponents and radicals

Powers built from repeated multiplication, the rules that follow from that, roots as a measurement question, and fractional powers.

In preparation  ·  Sessions 4–5  ·  Worksheets 4 and 5

Unit 3

Expressions, equations and formulas

Letters as short names for numbers, collecting and expanding, solving equations by balance, rearranging a formula, and turning a farm problem into one.

In preparation  ·  Sessions 6–7  ·  Worksheets 6 and 7

Unit 4

Inequalities and tolerance

Specifications and limits written as mathematics — at least, no more than, within. Solving them, drawing them, and the one rule that catches everybody.

In preparation  ·  Sessions 8–9  ·  Worksheets 8 and 9

Unit 5

Coordinate geometry and graphing

Plotting points, drawing the graph of a relationship, reading values off a line, and what gradient and intercept mean on a cost or growth graph.

In preparation  ·  Sessions 10–11

Unit 6

Sequences and series

Patterns that grow by a fixed amount or a fixed factor — loan repayments, depreciation on equipment, compound interest, pest populations.

In preparation  ·  Sessions 14–15

Unit 7

Matrices, polynomials and factorisation

Tables of figures handled as one object, multiplying out and the reverse of it, and factoring as the step before solving.

In preparation  ·  Sessions 16–18

Unit 8

Quadratics, trigonometry, geometry and calculus

Fencing the largest area for the length you have, heights and distances you cannot measure directly, and a first look at rate of change.

In preparation  ·  Sessions 19–24
