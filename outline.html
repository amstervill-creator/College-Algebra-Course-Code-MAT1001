About the course

Description

This course introduces the basic conceptual foundations of mathematics required for agricultural studies and related technical fields. It examines number systems, arithmetic operations, exponents, radicals, algebraic expressions, linear equations, inequalities, coordinate geometry, sequences and series, matrices, polynomials, factorisation, quadratic equations, trigonometry, geometry, and an introduction to calculus.

Emphasis is placed on interpreting mathematical symbols, solving problems, graphing linear relationships, applying algebraic methods, and using mathematical reasoning in agricultural and real-world situations. You will strengthen your numeracy, problem-solving, analytical and quantitative skills for farm calculations, measurements, data interpretation, production planning, financial decision-making, and further agricultural studies.

Why it matters

Farmers, technicians, agribusiness workers and agricultural managers regularly calculate measurements, areas, production rates, yields, costs, fertiliser applications, feed quantities, loan repayments, depreciation, profit, and other operational figures. College Algebra gives you the foundation to solve these problems accurately and confidently.

It also prepares you for later courses in the programme that rely on calculation and data — farm records, engineering principles, economics, soil science, animal nutrition, crop production, and farm business management.

Institutional context

This course contributes to the mission and vision of ABCAS by promoting academic excellence, innovation, workforce preparedness, and service to national and regional development, and supports the College’s commitment to developing graduates who contribute meaningfully to society and adapt to changing professional environments.

Aims

The course aims to:

  • Strengthen your understanding of number systems and arithmetic properties.
  • Develop your ability to simplify algebraic expressions and solve equations and inequalities.
  • Introduce coordinate geometry, matrices, polynomials, and quadratic equations.
  • Develop your understanding of sequences and series and their use in modelling growth, repayment, and depreciation.
  • Develop your understanding of trigonometry, geometry, and introductory calculus.
  • Promote mathematical problem-solving in agricultural and real-world contexts.
  • Build your confidence with mathematical terminology, notation, and procedures.
  • Prepare you to apply mathematics in agricultural science, farm business, and technical decision-making.

Learning outcomes

On successful completion of this course you will be able to:

  1. Identify and use arithmetic properties of integers, rational numbers, irrational numbers, and real numbers.
  2. Apply rules of exponents, radicals, roots, and algebraic operations.
  3. Solve linear equations and inequalities, including problems involving absolute values.
  4. Graph linear equations and determine x- and y-intercepts.
  5. Recognise and use arithmetic and geometric sequences and series, including nth terms and sums, to model agricultural, growth, and financial situations.
  6. Perform basic matrix operations.
  7. Simplify, compute, and factor monomials and polynomials.
  8. Solve quadratic equations using appropriate methods, including the quadratic formula.
  9. Apply basic trigonometry, geometry, and introductory calculus to agricultural and practical situations.
  10. Communicate mathematical reasoning, methods, and results using correct terminology and notation in written, symbolic, visual, and oral form.

What is covered

The course has six topics. On this site they are split into eight units so each one is a manageable size.

Topic 1 — Number systems, exponents and radicals

Integers, rational, irrational and real numbers; arithmetic operations; closure properties; rules of exponents; radicals and roots; fractional powers; reciprocals, opposites, and simplification.

On this site: Unit 1 and Unit 2

Topic 2 — Linear relations and inequalities

Algebraic expressions, linear equations, linear inequalities, absolute value equations and inequalities, word problems, multistep problem-solving, notation, and reasoning.

On this site: Unit 3 and Unit 4

Topic 3 — Coordinate geometry and graphing

The Cartesian plane, graphing linear equations, slope, straight-line relationships, x- and y-intercepts, and applications to agricultural measurements and data.

On this site: Unit 5

Topic 4 — Sequences and series

Number patterns; arithmetic and geometric sequences and series; the nth term; the sum of the first n terms; simple and compound growth. Applications include loan repayment schedules, straight-line and reducing-balance depreciation of farm equipment, compound interest on farm credit, pest and livestock population increase, and incremental yield gains.

On this site: Unit 6

Topic 5 — Matrices, polynomials and factorisation

Introduction to matrices and basic matrix operations; monomials and polynomials; operations with polynomials; factoring second-degree and simple third-degree polynomials.

On this site: Unit 7

Topic 6 — Quadratics, trigonometry, geometry and introductory calculus

Quadratic equations and the quadratic formula, basic trigonometry, geometry, agricultural applications, introductory differentiation and integration, and practical applications.

On this site: Unit 8

Teaching and resources

How the course is taught

Lectures, discussions, group work, demonstrations, case studies, presentations, practical activities, online activities, research activities, peer review, and problem-solving sessions.

Teaching includes worked examples, board demonstrations, guided practice, individual problem-solving, group activities, agriculture-based calculations, practice worksheets, and review exercises. The second hour of every Friday session is a supervised problem-solving session. You will be encouraged to show all working clearly and to explain the reasoning behind each step.

Required texts

  • Course notes and practice worksheets provided by the lecturer.
  • A college algebra textbook or reader selected by the department.

Recommended reading

Other resources

LMS resources, multimedia content, websites, handouts, practice worksheets, calculator activities, graph paper, and agricultural calculation exercises.

Policies

Attendance

You must attend at least 80% of scheduled classes, tutorials, workshops and other required academic activities. Students who do not meet the minimum may be prevented from sitting the final examination.

Late work

Work must be submitted by the stated deadline. 5% is deducted for each calendar day an assignment is late, for up to six days. Work more than six days late receives zero, unless an extension has been approved for documented exceptional circumstances.

Academic integrity

You are expected to uphold the highest standards of academic honesty. Work you submit must be your own and must properly acknowledge the ideas, words, research and contributions of others. Academic misconduct includes, but is not limited to:

  • plagiarism;
  • cheating in examinations, tests, quizzes, or other assessments;
  • collusion or unauthorised collaboration;
  • fabricating or falsifying information or data;
  • unauthorised use of artificial intelligence tools or digital resources where prohibited;
  • any other act that compromises academic honesty and ethical conduct.

You are responsible for knowing the College’s policies on academic integrity, ethical conduct, assessment regulations, and student responsibilities.

Technology and artificial intelligence

You may use approved AI tools to support your learning where the lecturer permits it. Any use of AI in assignments, projects, presentations, reports or other submissions must be clearly declared, and must not replace your own thinking, analysis, originality, or responsibility for the work.

You may not submit AI-generated work as entirely your own, use AI for plagiarism or dishonesty, use AI during examinations unless expressly authorised, falsify research or references using AI, or use it in any way that breaches the College’s academic integrity policies.
