5.1 The coordinate plane

How would you tell someone exactly where a point is, using only numbers?

Two numbers: how far along, then how far up. That pair is the point’s address, and the order never changes — along the corridor first, then up the stairs.

Reading an address

(3, 4): 3 along to the right, 4 up. The horizontal number is always first.

(−2, 3): 2 to the left, 3 up. A negative first number goes left; a negative second number goes down.

(0, 6) is on the y-axis; (−4, 0) is on the x-axis. A zero means you do not move in that direction at all.

5.2 From a rule to a table of values

y = 2x + 1 is a rule: double the number and add one.

Choose some values of x, put each one through the rule, and write down what comes out. That is substitution from Unit 3, done a few times.

Table for y = 2x + 1

x = −1:  2(−1) + 1 = −1 Put negatives in brackets.

x = 0:  2(0) + 1 = 1

x = 1:  2(1) + 1 = 3

x = 2:  2(2) + 1 = 5

The points are (−1, −1), (0, 1), (1, 3), (2, 5).

Is a point on the line?

Is (2, 7) on y = 3x + 1?

3(2) + 1 = 7. The y-value matches, so yes. Put the x in; see if the y comes out.

5.3 Drawing the line and reading from it

Three points, one ruler.

Plot at least three points from your table. If they lie in a straight line, rule through all of them and carry the line to the edge of the grid. If one is out of line, your table has a mistake — check it before you draw.

Reading a value off the line

On the graph of y = 2x − 1, what is y when x = 1.5?

Go along to 1.5, straight up to the line, then across to the y-axis.

Read y = 2. The table never had x = 1.5. The line filled in the gap.

5.4 Intercepts

Where does the line cross the axes?

Every point on the y-axis has x = 0, and every point on the x-axis has y = 0. So to find where a line crosses an axis, set the other letter to zero.

Both intercepts of 2x + 3y = 12

y-intercept: put x = 0. 3y = 12, so y = 4. The point is (0, 4). Crossing the y-axis: x is zero.

x-intercept: put y = 0. 2x = 12, so x = 6. The point is (6, 0).

Plot the two intercepts and rule the line. The quickest way to draw a line written like this.

5.5 Slope

The seedling grew 5 cm every week.

That steady 5 cm a week is the slope of the growth line: how much y changes for every 1 along. Rise over run.

Slope from two points

(1, 3) and (4, 9)

Rise: 9 − 3 = 6.  Run: 4 − 1 = 3. Take the points in the same order, top and bottom.

m = 6 ÷ 3 = 2

Positive slope: rises left to right. Negative: falls. Zero: flat. A vertical line has no slope — the run is zero.

5.6 The equation y = mx + c

Most straight lines can be written as y = mx + c.

m is the slope and c is where the line crosses the y-axis. Read them straight off the equation.

Reading m and c

y = 3x − 5: slope 3, y-intercept −5.

y = 5 + 3x: slope 3, y-intercept 5. The slope is the number with x, wherever it is written.

2y = 6x + 8: divide by 2 first, y = 3x + 4. It must say y = before you read it.

Finding the equation

Slope 3, through (2, 7).

Start with y = 3x + c.

Put the point in: 7 = 3(2) + c, so c = 1.

y = 3x + 1

5.7 Break-even: where two lines meet

Growing tomatoes costs $400 up front plus $3 for every kilogram. They sell for $5 a kilogram.

Cost: C = 3q + 400. The 400 is the fixed cost (the intercept); the 3 is the cost per kilogram (the slope). Revenue: R = 5q.

Finding break-even

Break-even is where the money in equals the money out: R = C.

5q = 3q + 400

2q = 400

q = 200 kg Sell fewer and you lose money; sell more and you make a profit.

Worksheet 11 · Section E — Costs, growth and break-even on the farm

For each one, say in words what the slope and the intercept mean, with their units. A number on its own is not an answer.

  1. The cost of growing q kg of tomatoes is C = 3q + 400 dollars. What do the 3 and the 400 mean?

    Show a hint

    One number grows with every kilogram; the other is paid even if you grow nothing.

  2. The tomatoes sell for $5 a kilogram, so the revenue is R = 5q. How many kilograms must be sold to break even?

    Show a hint

    Break-even means the money in equals the money out. Set 5q equal to 3q + 400.

  3. What is the profit if 350 kg are sold?

    Show a hint

    Work out the revenue and the cost separately, then subtract.

  4. A calf weighs 40 kg at birth (week 0) and 64 kg at week 8. Assuming steady growth, find its growth rate.

    Show a hint

    This is a slope: change in weight over change in weeks.

  5. Write an equation for the calf’s weight W after t weeks, and use it to predict its weight at week 12.

    Show a hint

    The slope is your answer to question 4; the intercept is the weight at week 0.

  6. The water in a tank after t hours is V = 900 − 60t litres. Find both intercepts and say what each means.

    Show a hint

    Put t = 0 for one intercept and V = 0 for the other.

Watch out for these

Reversing the pair
Plotting (3, 5) as (5, 3). Along first, then up — say it aloud before the pencil moves.
An uneven or unlabelled scale
If the steps are not equal, every reading off the graph is wrong. Label the origin 0 first, then equal steps.
Plotting only two points
Any two points make a straight line, so a mistake cannot show. Plot three.
Run over rise
Slope is rise ÷ run: the change in y on top.
Setting y = 0 to find the y-intercept
It is the other way round. On the y-axis, x = 0.
Giving a slope with no meaning
“3” is not an answer on a cost graph. “$3 for every extra kilogram” is.

Before you move on

You should be able to plot points in all four quadrants, draw a straight line from a table, find its intercepts and slope, write it as y = mx + c, and say what the slope and intercept mean on a farm graph.

The slope comes back in Unit 8 as the rate of change, which is where calculus begins.
