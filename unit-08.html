8.1 Solving quadratics by factorising

If two numbers multiply to give zero, what can you say about them?

One of them must be zero. That is the whole method: get zero on one side, factorise the other, and set each bracket to zero.

x2 + 5x + 6 = 0

(x + 2)(x + 3) = 0 Factorise exactly as in Unit 7.

x + 2 = 0  or  x + 3 = 0

x = −2 or x = −3 The sign changes when you read the root from the bracket.

Check: (−2)2 + 5(−2) + 6 = 4 − 10 + 6 = 0 ✓

8.2 Rearranging first

The method only works when one side is zero.

If the equation is x2 = 3x + 10, move everything to one side first.

x2 = 3x + 10

x2 − 3x − 10 = 0

(x − 5)(x + 2) = 0

x = 5 or x = −2

Never divide by x

x2 = 5x

x2 − 5x = 0,  x(x − 5) = 0 Take x out as a factor.

x = 0 or x = 5 Dividing both sides by x would have lost x = 0.

8.3 The quadratic formula

Not every quadratic factorises neatly.

x2 + 4x + 1 = 0 has no whole-number factors. The quadratic formula works on every quadratic, neat or not.

x = [−b ± √(b2 − 4ac)] ÷ 2a

x2 − 5x + 6 = 0:  a = 1, b = −5, c = 6 Write a, b and c with their signs first.

b2 − 4ac = 25 − 24 = 1 Work out the part under the root on its own.

x = (5 ± 1) ÷ 2 −b is +5, because b is −5.

x = 3 or x = 2

The discriminant b2 − 4ac tells you how many answers

Positive: two answers.

Zero: one repeated answer.

Negative: no real answers. You cannot take the square root of a negative number.

8.4 Answers to two decimal places

Most real problems give answers that are not whole numbers.

Use the formula exactly as in 8.3, work out the square root on the calculator, and round only at the very end.

x2 + 4x + 1 = 0

a = 1, b = 4, c = 1.  b2 − 4ac = 16 − 4 = 12

x = (−4 ± √12) ÷ 2 = (−4 ± 3.4641) ÷ 2

x = −0.27 or x = −3.73 Divide the whole top by 2a, not just the root.

8.5 Fencing the largest area

You have 100 m of fencing for a rectangular pen. Which shape holds the most?

If the width is x, the two widths use 2x, leaving 100 − 2x for the two lengths. So each length is 50 − x, and the area is A = x(50 − x).

Try some widths

x = 10:  10 × 40 = 400 m²

x = 20:  20 × 30 = 600 m²

x = 25:  25 × 25 = 625 m²

x = 30:  30 × 20 = 600 m²

The table goes up, peaks at 25, and comes down the same way. The largest pen is a square.

8.6 Perimeter and area

Fencing goes round a field; fertiliser covers it.

Perimeter is the distance round (metres). Area is the ground covered (square metres). Decide which one the question wants before you calculate.

The shapes you need

Rectangle: P = 2(l + w),  A = l × w

Triangle: A = ½ × base × height

Circle: C = 2πr,  A = πr2 r is the radius — half the diameter. Halve a diameter before you use it.

A circular pen of radius 7 m: C = 2 × π × 7 = 43.98 m of fence; A = π × 49 = 153.94 m² of ground.

Reference — formulas and conversions (from Worksheet 21)

8.7 Volume and converting units

How much water does the tank hold?

Volume is the space inside, in cubic metres. For a box multiply length, width and height. For a cylinder (a tank or silo) it is the area of the circle times the height.

A cylindrical tank, radius 1.5 m, height 2 m

V = πr2h = π × 1.52 × 2 = 14.14 m³

× 1,000 = about 14,140 litres 1 m³ holds 1,000 litres.

Converting area

1 m = 100 cm, so 1 m² = 100 × 100 = 10,000 cm² Square the length factor for area; cube it for volume.

1 hectare = 10,000 m²

8.8 Pythagoras’ theorem

A plot is 120 m by 50 m. How long is a pipe laid corner to corner?

The diagonal makes a right-angled triangle with two sides of the plot. In any right-angled triangle, the two shorter sides squared add up to the longest side squared.

a2 + b2 = c2

c2 = 1202 + 502 = 14,400 + 2,500 = 16,900 c is the hypotenuse, the side opposite the right angle.

c = √16,900 = 130 m

Finding a shorter side: subtract instead. A 6 m ladder with its foot 1.5 m out: height = √(36 − 2.25) = 5.81 m.

8.9 Trigonometry: SOH CAH TOA

You stand 20 m from a silo and look up at 52° to the top. How tall is it?

You cannot climb it, but a right-angled triangle and one angle are enough. First name the sides from the angle you are using.

The three ratios

sin θ = opposite ÷ hypotenuse   SOH

cos θ = adjacent ÷ hypotenuse   CAH

tan θ = opposite ÷ adjacent   TOA

The silo

You know the adjacent (20 m) and want the opposite (the height): use tan. Pick the ratio that uses the side you know and the side you want.

tan 52° = h ÷ 20

h = 20 × tan 52° = 25.60 m

Finding an angle

Opposite 5, hypotenuse 10: sin θ = 0.5

θ = sin−1(0.5) = 30° Use the inverse (shift) key only when you are finding an angle.

Check the calculator says D or DEG before you start. In radian mode every answer is wrong.

8.10 Rate of change: the derivative

A plant was 12 cm on day 10 and 30 cm on day 16. How fast was it growing?

On average, 18 cm in 6 days: 3 cm a day. That is a slope, from Unit 5. But growth is rarely steady — the derivative gives the rate at one moment.

The power rule

y = x5  →  dy/dx = 5x4 Bring the power down in front, then take 1 off the power.

y = 4x3  →  dy/dx = 4 × 3x2 = 12x2

y = 6x  →  6;  y = 10  →  0 A number on its own never changes, so its rate is 0.

8.11 Differentiating polynomials, and the rate at a point

Differentiate one term at a time.

Then put in the value you are interested in to get the rate at that moment.

y = x2 + 3x at x = 2

dy/dx = 2x + 3 x2 gives 2x; 3x gives 3.

At x = 2: 2(2) + 3 = 7 At that point y is rising 7 for every 1 along.

A crop: h = 0.5t2 + 2t cm after t weeks

dh/dt = t + 2

Week 4: 4 + 2 = 6 cm a week The rate. The height itself comes from h, not from dh/dt.

8.12 Integration: from a rate back to a total

Rain falls at 2 mm an hour for 5 hours. How much falls?

10 mm — rate times time. When the rate changes, integration does the same job: it turns a rate into a total. It is differentiation run backwards.

The power rule backwards

∫x3 dx = x4/4 + C Add 1 to the power, then divide by the new power.

∫6x dx = 6x2/2 + C = 3x2 + C + C, because any number would have disappeared when differentiating.

A total over a time

∫ from 0 to 2 of 3x2 dx

Integrate: x3

Top limit minus bottom limit: 23 − 03 = 8 With limits there is no + C.

These are the questions that count.

Section E of each worksheet. They are marked and discussed in class, so the answers are not given here. Write each one out on paper first. If you are stuck, open the hint — it gives the first step, not the answer.

Worksheet 19 · Section E — Plots, rows and break-even

Write the equation, get zero on one side, factorise and solve. Then decide which solution makes sense — a length or a number of rows cannot be negative.

  1. A rectangular plot is 3 m longer than it is wide, and its area is 40 m². Using x for the width, x(x + 3) = 40. Find the width and the length.

    Show a hint

    Multiply out, move the 40 across, then factorise. One answer will not make sense.

  2. A pen is x m wide and (x + 2) m long, with an area of 48 m². Find its dimensions.

    Show a hint

    Write x(x + 2) = 48, then get zero on one side.

  3. A field measures (x + 4) m by (x + 1) m and has an area of 70 m². Find x and the field’s dimensions.

    Show a hint

    Expand the brackets first, then take 70 from both sides.

  4. A grower’s profit is P = −x² + 14x − 40 hundred dollars when x hundred kilograms are sold. Find the two quantities at which the grower breaks even (P = 0).

    Show a hint

    Set P = 0 and multiply every term by −1 so the x² is positive.

  5. Seedlings are planted in n rows with (n + 4) seedlings in each row. There are 96 seedlings altogether. How many rows are there?

    Show a hint

    Rows × seedlings per row = 96.

Worksheet 20 · Section E — Fencing and optimisation

Draw a sketch and label it before you write anything else. Decide which answers make sense on the ground.

  1. A farmer has 100 m of fencing for a rectangular pen. If the width is x m, the length is (50 − x) m. Find the area when x = 10, 20, 25 and 30.

    Show a hint

    Area = width × length = x(50 − x). Put each value of x in.

  2. Which width gives the largest area? What shape is the pen then?

    Show a hint

    Look at your table. What do you notice about the length when x = 25?

  3. For the same 100 m of fencing, find the width that gives an area of exactly 600 m².

    Show a hint

    Set x(50 − x) = 600 and get zero on one side.

  4. A pen is built against a barn wall, so 60 m of fencing makes only three sides. With width x m, the length is (60 − 2x) m. Find the width if the area is to be 400 m².

    Show a hint

    Two widths and one length use the fencing. Area = x(60 − 2x).

  5. The milk from a pasture is Y = 40n − 2n² litres a day when n cows graze it. Find the two values of n for which Y = 0, then use the point halfway between them to find the number of cows that gives the most milk.

    Show a hint

    Take out the common factor 2n. The best value is midway between the two roots.

  6. A plot is 4 m longer than it is wide and has an area of 60 m². Use the quadratic formula to find its dimensions.

    Show a hint

    Write x(x + 4) = 60, get zero on one side, then use a = 1, b = 4, c = −60.

Worksheet 21 · Section E — Fields, tanks and silos

Decide first: is the question about distance round (perimeter), ground covered (area) or space inside (volume)? Then check your units match.

  1. A field measures 250 m by 160 m. Find its area in hectares.

    Show a hint

    Find the area in m², then divide by 10,000.

  2. Fertiliser is spread on that field at 150 kg per hectare. How much is needed?

    Show a hint

    Rate × area, with the area in hectares.

  3. A cylindrical water tank has a radius of 1.5 m and a height of 2 m. Find its capacity in litres.

    Show a hint

    Volume of a cylinder in m³, then × 1,000 for litres.

  4. Each cow drinks 60 litres a day. For how many full days will the full tank in question 3 supply 20 cows?

    Show a hint

    Find the daily total for the herd first.

  5. A silo is a cylinder of radius 2.5 m and height 8 m. Grain weighs 750 kg per cubic metre. What mass of grain does the full silo hold?

    Show a hint

    Find the volume, then multiply by the kilograms in each cubic metre.

  6. A field 200 m by 150 m is to be fenced with 4 strands of wire. How much wire is needed?

    Show a hint

    This is a perimeter question. How many times does the wire go round?

Worksheet 22 · Section E — Heights and distances on the farm

Sketch the triangle, mark the right angle, label the three sides from the angle you know, then choose the ratio. Give units.

  1. You stand 20 m from the base of a silo. The angle of elevation to the top is 52°. How tall is the silo?

    Show a hint

    You know the adjacent side and want the opposite. Which ratio uses both?

  2. An irrigation pipe runs diagonally across a rectangular plot 120 m by 50 m. How long is the pipe?

    Show a hint

    The diagonal is the hypotenuse. Use Pythagoras.

  3. A 6 m ladder leans against a barn wall with its foot 1.5 m from the wall. How high up the wall does it reach?

    Show a hint

    The ladder is the hypotenuse. Subtract, do not add.

  4. A field rises 15 m over a horizontal distance of 200 m. What is the angle of slope?

    Show a hint

    Rise is opposite, horizontal distance is adjacent. Use tan⁻¹.

  5. A tree casts a shadow 18 m long when the sun is at an elevation of 40°. How tall is the tree?

    Show a hint

    The shadow is along the ground (adjacent). The tree is opposite the angle.

  6. A wire runs from the top of a 9 m pole to the ground and makes an angle of 65° with the ground. How long is the wire?

    Show a hint

    The pole is opposite the 65° angle and the wire is the hypotenuse.

Worksheet 23 · Section E — Growth rates on the farm

A derivative is a rate: kilograms per week, centimetres per day, litres per day. Give the unit every time.

  1. A crop’s height after t weeks is h = 0.5t² + 2t centimetres. Find a formula for its growth rate.

    Show a hint

    Differentiate term by term: 0.5t² and 2t separately.

  2. Find the growth rate at week 4 and at week 8.

    Show a hint

    Put t = 4, then t = 8, into your answer to question 1.

  3. How tall is the crop at week 4? (This is not the same question as question 2.)

    Show a hint

    Use the original formula for h, not the derivative.

  4. A piglet’s mass after t weeks is m = 2 + t + 0.3t² kilograms. How fast is it gaining mass at week 5?

    Show a hint

    Differentiate, then put t = 5 in. The 2 disappears.

  5. The profit from selling x kg of a crop is P = 60x − x² dollars. Find the value of x at which the rate of change of profit is zero, and the profit there.

    Show a hint

    Set dP/dx = 0 and solve. Then put that x into P.

  6. The water in a pond after t days is V = 500 − 4t² litres. Find the rate of change at day 3 and say what it means.

    Show a hint

    Differentiate, put t = 3 in, then explain what a negative rate means.

Worksheet 24 · Section E — Totals from rates on the farm

If you know how fast something is happening, integrating gives the total over the time. Give units.

  1. Rain falls at a steady 2 mm an hour for 5 hours. How much rain falls in total?

    Show a hint

    Rate × time. Then check it as an integral of 2 from 0 to 5.

  2. A dairy’s production rate is R = 40 + 2t litres a day, t days after a new feed is introduced. Find the total produced over the first 10 days.

    Show a hint

    Integrate 40 + 2t, then put in 10 and 0.

  3. During a storm, rain falls at r = 3t² mm a day for days 0 to 2. Find the total rainfall.

    Show a hint

    Integrate 3t², then use the limits 0 and 2.

  4. A calf gains mass at a rate of dm/dt = 0.6t + 1 kg a week. It weighs 30 kg at birth (t = 0). Find a formula for its mass m.

    Show a hint

    Integrate, then use the birth weight to find C.

  5. Use your formula from question 4 to find the calf’s mass at week 10.

    Show a hint

    Put t = 10 into your formula.

  6. Say whether you would differentiate or integrate to answer each: (a) how fast is the crop growing in week 6? (b) how much water flowed into the tank during the day, given the flow rate?

    Show a hint

    Does it ask for a rate or for a total?

Watch out for these

Using the zero product rule without a zero
(x − 2)(x − 3) = 2 does not mean x − 2 = 2. Get zero on one side first.
Dividing by x and losing x = 0
In x2 = 5x, take x out as a factor instead.
Dropping the signs in the formula
If b = −6, then −b = +6 and b2 = +36. Write a, b and c with their signs first.
Keeping a negative length
A field cannot be −8 m wide. Say which answer you are rejecting and why.
Converting area by the length factor
1 m² is 10,000 cm², not 100 cm².
Using the diameter as the radius
Halve the diameter first.
Naming opposite and adjacent from the right angle
Name them from the angle you are using.
Calculator in radian mode
Look for D or DEG on the screen before any trigonometry.
Lowering the power without bringing it down
x3 differentiates to 3x2, not x2. And a constant differentiates to 0.
Forgetting + C
Every integral without limits needs + C.
