6.1 Patterns and arithmetic sequences

A tractor costs $60,000 and loses $7,500 of value each year.

Its values go $60,000, $52,500, $45,000, $37,500… The same amount comes off each time. A list that changes by the same amount each step is an arithmetic sequence.

Is it arithmetic?

4, 9, 14, 19

9 − 4 = 5,  14 − 9 = 5,  19 − 14 = 5 Take each term minus the one before it.

The same every time, so it is arithmetic with d = 5. d is the common difference. It is negative if the list falls.

2, 4, 8, 16 is not arithmetic. The differences are 2, 4, 8 — not the same.

6.2 The nth term

What is the 50th term of 5, 8, 11, …? Nobody wants to write out fifty numbers.

Look at how each term is built from the first one: 5, then 5 + 3, then 5 + 3 + 3. Term 50 is the first term plus 49 threes.

The formula: an = a + (n − 1)d

5, 8, 11, …   a = 5, d = 3

a10 = 5 + (10 − 1) × 3 = 5 + 27 = 32 n − 1, not n: the first term has no d added yet.

Formula for any term: 5 + 3(n − 1) = 3n + 2 Check: n = 1 gives 5 ✓

Working backwards

a3 = 11 and a7 = 23.

From term 3 to term 7 is 4 steps, so 4d = 12 and d = 3. Count the steps between the terms.

Go back 2 steps from a3: a = 11 − 6 = 5.

6.3 Adding up: arithmetic series

A loan is repaid at $200, then $220, then $240, rising $20 a month. How much over a year?

You want the total, not one month. Adding the terms of a sequence gives a series.

The pairing idea

1 + 2 + 3 + … + 100

Pair the first and last: 1 + 100 = 101. Then 2 + 99 = 101, and so on. Every pair makes the same total.

50 pairs of 101: 5,050

The formula: Sn = n ÷ 2 × [2a + (n − 1)d]

2 + 5 + 8 + … to 10 terms: a = 2, d = 3, n = 10

S10 = 10 ÷ 2 × [2(2) + 9(3)] = 5 × 31 = 155

If you know the last term: Sn = n ÷ 2 × (first + last). That is the pairing idea written as a formula.

6.4 Geometric sequences

A pest population of 500 grows by 30% every week.

Each week multiplies the number by 1.3. A list where each term is the one before multiplied by the same number is a geometric sequence.

Common ratio

3, 6, 12, 24

6 ÷ 3 = 2,  12 ÷ 6 = 2,  24 ÷ 12 = 2 Divide each term by the one before.

Geometric with r = 2.

The nth term: an = a × rn − 1

3, 6, 12, …   a = 3, r = 2

a6 = 3 × 25 = 3 × 32 = 96 The power is one less than the term number — the same reason as before.

6.5 Growth, interest and geometric series

A $10,000 farm loan grows at 5% a year compound interest.

After one year it is 10,000 × 1.05. After two, 10,000 × 1.05 × 1.05. The multiplier for 5% growth is 1.05 — not 5, and not 0.05.

Percentage to ratio

5% growth: r = 1 + 0.05 = 1.05 You keep the 100% and add 5%.

20% fall: r = 1 − 0.20 = 0.8 Losing 20% leaves 80%.

After 3 years: 10,000 × 1.053 = $11,576.25

Adding a geometric sequence: Sn = a(rn − 1) ÷ (r − 1)

2 + 6 + 18 + … to 5 terms: a = 2, r = 3

S5 = 2(35 − 1) ÷ (3 − 1) = 2 × 242 ÷ 2 = 242

One year or all the years? an gives the value in year n. Sn gives the total of all n years. Read the question twice.

Watch out for these

Using n instead of n − 1
The most common error in this topic. Check your formula gives the first term when n = 1.
Assuming every pattern is arithmetic
2, 4, 8, 16 has a visible pattern, but the differences are not equal. Check before using the formula.
Losing the minus sign on d
Depreciation falls, so d is negative: d = −7,500, not 7,500.
Writing r = 5 or r = 0.05 for 5% growth
The multiplier is 1.05. Ask whether your answer is believable — a loan does not grow to a million dollars in three years.
Using the arithmetic sum on a geometric series
Decide first: same amount each time (arithmetic) or same percentage each time (geometric)?
Giving the total when the question asks for one year
The value in year 5 is a term. The production over 5 years is a sum.

Before you move on

You should be able to tell an arithmetic sequence from a geometric one, find any term, add up a series of each kind, and turn a percentage growth or loss into the right multiplier.
