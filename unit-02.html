2.1 Powers: repeated multiplication

A pest population doubles every week.

Start with 2 insects. After 5 weeks there are 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 of them. Writing that out every time is tiring, so we write 25 instead.

Reading a power

34 = 3 × 3 × 3 × 3 = 81 The small number says how many 3s are multiplied together.

25 = 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 = 32 Not 2 × 5. That is the first trap.

y × y × y × y × y × y = y6

The rules, from writing it out

Multiplying: 23 × 24 = (2×2×2) × (2×2×2×2) = 27 Count the twos: 3 + 4 = 7. Add the powers.

Dividing: x6 ÷ x2 = x4 Two of the six x’s cancel. Subtract the powers.

Power of a power: (a3)4 = a3 × a3 × a3 × a3 = a12 Four lots of 3. Multiply the powers.

These rules only work when the base is the same. 23 × 32 cannot be collected into one power.

2.2 Zero and negative powers

Follow the pattern down.

23 = 8, 22 = 4, 21 = 2. Each step down divides by 2. Keep going and the pattern decides what the next ones must be.

Continuing the pattern

21 = 2

20 = 2 ÷ 2 = 1 Anything (except 0) to the power 0 is 1.

2−1 = 1 ÷ 2 = 1/2

2−2 = 1/2 ÷ 2 = 1/4

So 2−2 = 1 ÷ 22. A negative power means “one over”. It never makes the answer negative.

2.3 Signs and brackets

Is −24 the same as (−2)4?

No — and the difference is the brackets. The power only applies to what is directly underneath it.

Watch the brackets

(−2)4 = (−2)(−2)(−2)(−2) = 16 Four negatives make a positive.

−24 = −(2 × 2 × 2 × 2) = −16 Only the 2 is raised to the power; the minus waits outside.

(2x3)2 = 22 × x6 = 4x6 Everything inside the bracket is squared — the 2 as well.

2.4 Scientific notation

A soil test reports 30,000 bacteria per gram. A pesticide label says 0.0025 grams per kilogram.

Numbers that large or that small are easy to misread by a zero. Scientific notation writes them as a number between 1 and 10 times a power of 10.

Large numbers

45,000 = 4.5 × 10,000

= 4.5 × 104 The point moved 4 places to the left, so the power is 4.

Small numbers

0.00045 = 4.5 ÷ 10,000

= 4.5 × 10−4 The point moved 4 places to the right, so the power is −4.

Multiplying

(3 × 104) × 200 = (3 × 104) × (2 × 102)

= 6 × 106 Multiply the numbers, add the powers of ten.

2.5 Square roots and cube roots

A square plot has an area of 144 m². How long is each side?

You need the number that, multiplied by itself, gives 144. That is what a square root asks.

Roots undo powers

√144 = 12, because 12 × 12 = 144 The table of squares below is worth keeping in front of you.

∛64 = 4, because 4 × 4 × 4 = 64 A cube root asks for three equal numbers multiplied together — a cube-shaped tank.

√((−5)2) = √25 = 5 Work out the bracket first.

2.6 Simplifying and combining surds

√50 is not a whole number. But it can be written more simply.

Look for the largest square number that divides into it, and take its root out to the front.

Simplifying

√50 = √(25 × 2) = √25 × √2

= 5√2 25 is the largest square number that divides 50.

Adding and subtracting

3√5 + 4√5 = 7√5 Seven of the same thing — exactly like 3x + 4x.

√20 + √45 = 2√5 + 3√5 = 5√5 Simplify each one first; then they match.

Multiplying

√6 × √8 = √48 = √(16 × 3) = 4√3

2.7 Fractional powers

What could 9½ mean?

Use the multiplying rule: 9½ × 9½ = 91 = 9. The number that times itself gives 9 is 3. So a power of ½ is a square root.

Reading a fractional power

x½ = √x    x⅓ = ∛x

82/3 = (∛8)2 = 22 = 4 The bottom of the fraction is the root; the top is the power. Take the root first — the numbers stay small.

16−½ = 1 ÷ √16 = 1/4 The minus still means “one over”.

Worksheet 5 · Section E — Sizing on the farm

These are measurement questions. Decide what shape you are dealing with and what you have been given before you calculate.

  1. A square plot has an area of 2,500 square metres. How long is one side?

    Show a hint

    Area = side × side. Which number times itself is 2,500?

  2. A cubic water tank holds 27 cubic metres. How long is one edge?

    Show a hint

    Volume of a cube = edge × edge × edge. Which number cubed is 27?

  3. A square field covers exactly 1 hectare (10,000 square metres). How long is one side, in metres?

    Show a hint

    Take the square root of 10,000.

  4. A rectangular plot measures 60 metres by 80 metres. A fence is to run diagonally from one corner to the opposite corner. How long must it be? (Use a² + b² = c².)

    Show a hint

    The fence is the long side of a right-angled triangle with shorter sides 60 and 80.

  5. A square plot covers 2.25 hectares. How long is one side, in metres? (1 hectare = 10,000 square metres.)

    Show a hint

    Change the hectares to square metres first.

Watch out for these

Multiplying the base by the power
25 is 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 = 32, not 2 × 5 = 10.
Multiplying the powers when they should be added
23 × 24 = 27, not 212. Write it out and count.
Collecting powers with different bases
23 × 32 = 8 × 9 = 72. It is not 65.
Reading a negative power as a negative number
2−3 = 1/8. Nothing about it is negative.
Losing the brackets on a negative
−24 = −16, but (−2)4 = 16.
Splitting a root across a sum
√(9 + 16) = √25 = 5, not √9 + √16 = 7. Roots do not split over + or −.

Before you move on

You should be able to work out a power, use the three rules on the same base, handle zero and negative powers, write a number in scientific notation, and simplify a root.
