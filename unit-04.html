4.1 Writing and solving inequalities

A seed lot is accepted only if germination is at least 85%.

Not exactly 85 — anything from 85 up. That is g ≥ 85. An inequality describes a whole range of numbers, not just one.

Solving works like an equation

x + 4 > 9

Subtract 4 from both sides: x > 5

Check with a number in the range: x = 6 gives 10 > 9 ✓ Any number bigger than 5 works.

3x + 2 ≥ 14  →  3x ≥ 12  →  x ≥ 4

4.2 When the sign turns round

3 is less than 5. Is −3 less than −5?

No: on a number line −3 is to the right of −5, so −3 is bigger. Multiplying both sides by a negative flips the order, so the sign must turn round.

Dividing by a negative

−4x < 12

Divide both sides by −4 and turn the sign round: x > −3 Only when you multiply or divide by a negative. Adding or subtracting never turns it.

Check with x = 0: −4(0) = 0, and 0 < 12 ✓ Zero is bigger than −3, so it should work — and it does.

4.3 Number lines and interval notation

A picture of the answer.

Draw a number line. An open circle means the boundary is not included (< or >); a filled circle means it is (≤ or ≥). Then an arrow in the direction of the solution.

Interval notation

x > 3 is written (3, ∞) Round bracket: 3 is not included. Infinity always gets a round bracket.

x ≤ −1 is written (−∞, −1]

2 < x ≤ 5 is written (2, 5] Square bracket: 5 is included.

4.4 Absolute value as distance

How far is −5 from zero?

Five — the same as 5. The bars | | measure a distance, and a distance has no direction. Do not think “make it positive”; think “how far from zero”.

Reading the bars

|9| = 9   and   |−9| = 9

|4 − 11| = |−7| = 7 The bars are brackets too: work out the inside first.

|x − a| is the distance between x and a. So the gap between 3 and 10 is |3 − 10| = 7.

Reference — what the bars mean (from Worksheet 9)

|x|How far x is from zero. A distance, so it is never negative.
|x − a|How far x is from a — the gap between the two points, whichever way round you subtract.
|x| = 6Two answers, 6 and −6 — one on each side of zero.
|x| < 4Within 4 of zero: one unbroken band, −4 < x < 4.
|x| > 4More than 4 from zero: two separate pieces, x < −4 or x > 4.

4.5 Absolute value equations

Which points on the line are 6 away from zero?

Two of them: 6 and −6, one on each side. That is why an absolute value equation usually has two answers.

Two cases, every time

|x − 2| = 6

Case 1: x − 2 = 6, so x = 8

Case 2: x − 2 = −6, so x = −4

x = 8 or x = −4 Check both: |8 − 2| = 6 ✓ and |−4 − 2| = 6 ✓

|x| = −4 has no solution. Nothing can be a negative distance from zero.

4.6 “Within” and “further than”

A bag should weigh 50 kg and may be out by up to 2 kg.

Acceptable bags weigh from 48 to 52 kg — you knew that without algebra. Written down, it is |w − 50| ≤ 2: the distance from 50 is at most 2.

Within: one band

|x| < 4 means within 4 of zero

−4 < x < 4 One unbroken stretch of the number line.

Further than: two pieces

|x| > 4 means more than 4 from zero

x < −4 or x > 4 Two separate pieces with a gap between. It needs the word “or”.

Worksheet 8 · Section E — Limits and specifications on the farm

Write the inequality first, then solve it. Say what your answer means in words, with units.

  1. A seed lot is accepted only if its germination rate is at least 85%. Write this as an inequality using g. A batch tests at 84%. Is it accepted?

    Show a hint

    “At least” means ≥. Is 84 at least 85?

  2. Stocking density must not exceed 8 animals per hectare. A farmer has 7.5 hectares. Write an inequality for n, the number of animals, and find the largest number that may be kept.

    Show a hint

    Animals ÷ hectares must be no more than 8.

  3. A feed ration must contain at least 16% protein. A batch weighs 50 kg. Write an inequality for p, the mass of protein in kg, and find the minimum mass required.

    Show a hint

    16% of 50 kg is the smallest amount allowed.

  4. A farmer has $900 to spend on fertiliser costing $45 a bag. Write an inequality for b, the number of bags, and find how many may be bought.

    Show a hint

    45 times the number of bags must not go over 900.

  5. Grain may be stored only if its moisture content is below 14%. Write this as an inequality using m. A sample reads 13.8%. May it be stored?

    Show a hint

    “Below” means <. Is 13.8 below 14?

  6. A packing crate must weigh at least 20 kg but no more than 25 kg. Write this as a double inequality using w. A crate weighs 25 kg exactly. Does it pass?

    Show a hint

    Both ends are allowed. Does ≤ include 25?

Worksheet 9 · Section E — Tolerances on the farm

A tolerance says how far a measurement may be from its target. Write the absolute value inequality first, then solve it for the acceptable range, then answer the question in a sentence.

  1. A bag of feed should weigh 50 kg and may be out by up to 2 kg. Write this using w, and give the range of acceptable weights.

    Show a hint

    |w − 50| ≤ 2. Go 2 below 50 and 2 above.

  2. Soil pH must be within 0.3 of a target of 6.5. Write this using p, and give the acceptable range.

    Show a hint

    |p − 6.5| ≤ 0.3. Go 0.3 either side of 6.5.

  3. Stored grain should be at 13% moisture, within 1 percentage point. Write this using m and give the range. A sample reads 14.2% — does it pass?

    Show a hint

    Find the range 13 ± 1. Is 14.2 inside it?

  4. A crate is marked 25 kg with a tolerance of 0.4 kg. Write this using w and give the range. A crate weighs 24.5 kg — does it pass?

    Show a hint

    Find the range 25 ± 0.4. Check the lower end as well as the upper.

  5. A sprayer should deliver 200 litres per hectare, within 5 litres. Write this using d, and give the acceptable range.

    Show a hint

    Write |d − 200| ≤ 5, then go 5 either side.

  6. A seed batch is rejected if its germination rate differs from the stated 90% by more than 4 percentage points. Write the rejection condition using g, and state which germination rates are rejected.

    Show a hint

    “Differs by more than 4” is the “further than” type — two pieces.

Watch out for these

Forgetting to turn the sign round
When you multiply or divide both sides by a negative number, < becomes > and ≤ becomes ≥. Check with a number.
Turning the sign round when you should not
Adding or subtracting a negative never reverses the sign. Only multiplying or dividing does.
Mixing up < and ≤
“Below 14%” does not allow 14; “no more than 25 kg” does allow 25. The words decide.
Solving only one case of an absolute value equation
A distance of 6 is reached from two directions. Write both cases before you solve either.
Writing |x| > 5 as −5 < x < 5
Shade it on a number line first. It is two pieces with a gap, and needs “or”.
Splitting the bars across a sum
|3 + (−7)| = 4, but |3| + |−7| = 10. Work out the inside first.

Before you move on

You should be able to turn a limit in words into an inequality, solve it (turning the sign when you divide by a negative), show it on a number line and in interval notation, and write and solve a tolerance as an absolute value.
